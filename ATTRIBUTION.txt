Source recordings
=================

The two validation recordings are real human singing from the MTG essentia-audio
test corpus (github.com/MTG/essentia-audio), which collects Creative Commons
material from Freesound.

They are NOT committed to this repository. Run ./tools/fetch_test_audio.sh to
reproduce them exactly; the offsets and durations in that script are the ones
RESULTS.md's real-voice figures were measured on.

sustained_dry  — 5 s from the start of "long_voice" (recorded/long_voice.flac).
                 A soprano sustaining roughly 810 Hz with natural vibrato and
                 drift. The "continuous note" case: steady pitch, so grain
                 behaviour is isolated from tracking behaviour.

melody_dry     — 5 s from 30 s into a Carnatic varnam sung by Vignesh in Abhogi
                 raaga, Freesound ID 223582 by user gopalkoduri.
                 Roughly 210-320 Hz with clear melodic movement, plus tambura
                 and accompaniment in the background. Deliberately the harder
                 case: it is not a solo dry vocal, so the pitch tracker has
                 competing periodic material. This is the recording on which the
                 VH-002 clicking artefact reproduces.

Both are converted to mono 48 kHz, trimmed to 5 s, and loudness-normalised
before processing.

Licences are those of the original Freesound uploads; consult the essentia-audio
repository and the Freesound entries before redistributing the dry material.


Demo recordings (milestone 3)
=============================

Two further real voices, chosen because neither resembles the two validation
recordings above and because between them they cover the failure modes the
milestone-3 work targeted. Prepared by tools/fetch_test_audio.sh.

flamenco_dry   — 14 s of flamenco cante, from "flamenco.flac" in the MTG
                 essentia-audio corpus (Creative Commons material from
                 Freesound). Male, roughly 157-390 Hz, chest register, heavy
                 melisma and rapid pitch movement, with guitar behind the voice.

                 Chosen as the SINGING case. It is the hardest available
                 material for the epoch tracker and for VH-002: the pitch almost
                 never holds still.

speech_dry     — 30 s assembled from the Free Spoken Digit Dataset
                 (github.com/Jakobovski/free-spoken-digit-dataset, CC BY-SA 4.0),
                 speaker "theo", digits 0-9 in shuffled order with 120 ms gaps.
                 Male, roughly 110-190 Hz.

                 Chosen as the CONSONANT case, and it is assembled rather than
                 found because no recording in the corpus has this density of
                 plosives and fricatives. Spoken digits happen to cover the exact
                 phonemes the reported symptom named: /k/ and /s/ in "six", /t/
                 in "two" and "eight", /f/ in "four" and "five", /v/ in "seven",
                 /th/ in "three". A voiced-unvoiced boundary every few hundred
                 milliseconds is precisely the condition that made the old hard
                 switch audible.

                 CAVEAT, stated because it limits what this file can show: the
                 source is 8 kHz, so it carries no energy above 4 kHz. Sibilant
                 energy is therefore under-represented and the sibilant-collapse
                 figures measured on it are, if anything, conservative. It is
                 upsampled to 48 kHz for the pipeline, which does not put the
                 missing band back.

Licences are those of the original uploads. Consult the essentia-audio
repository, the Freesound entries and the FSDD repository before redistributing.
